Iron

Privacy policy

Last updated 1 November 2026

Iron is a personal training log. It works on your phone, with an optional copy in your own Google account.

What Iron stores

Your workouts, plans, bodyweight, measurements, food, water and settings are saved in a database on your phone. That database is encrypted.

What Iron sends

Nothing, unless you sign in with Google.

Iron has no server of its own and no analytics. It contains no ads, uses no advertising identifier and sends no crash reports.

If you sign in with Google

Your Google account becomes your Iron account. Iron keeps an encrypted copy of its database in a private folder inside your own Google Drive, called the app data folder, and refreshes it after a workout, when you put the app away with new data, and when you open it after a long gap. Only Iron can read that folder; it does not appear in your Drive and other apps cannot see it. The developer has no access to it.

The key that opens the copy is stored with it, so that signing in on a new phone brings everything back without a phrase to type. That means the copy is protected by your Google account: anyone who can sign in as you could restore it, so keep that account secure.

Signing in tells Iron the name and email address on your Google account. Both stay on your phone and are used only to show you which account is signed in.

Deleting your data

Delete all data, in the app under You → Backup & data, erases everything on your phone and, if you are signed in, the copy in your Google account. Signing out stops syncing and leaves your phone as it is. Uninstalling Iron removes everything on the phone; the Drive copy can also be removed in Google Drive's settings, under Manage apps → Iron → Delete hidden app data.

Never sold

Your data is never sold or shared. It is not used for advertising or profiling.

Iron is not directed at children under 13.
